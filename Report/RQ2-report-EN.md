# RQ-2 Experiment Report

## 1. Task Description

This experiment aims to test the ability of LLMs to retrieve relevant papers based on a given problem (Observation) and to generate corresponding solutions.

The experiment consists of three parts:

**RQ2-1**: Testing whether the LLM has the ability to generate corresponding related work.

Input: Observation

Output: Related works and core claim.

GroundTruth: human generated Related works and core claim.

**RQ2-2**: Testing the ability of the LLM to generate solutions

Input: Observation and related works (From human)

Output: Solution

GroundTruth: human generated solution.

**RQ2-3**: Test the influence of the LLM-generated "related work" and "human-related work" on generating the final solution.

Input: Observation and related works (generated by LLM) / Observation and related works (generated by human)

Output: Solutions

Target: Compare the impact of the two input pairs on the solution.

## 2. Experimental Procedure

1. **Paper information extraction**: The papers used are the ADRS tasks in the paper [Let the Barbarians In: How AI Can Accelerate Systems Performance Research](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.14806#page=19&zoom=100,430,470). (There are 10 tasks in total; two tasks come from the same paper and one task has no text paper, so 8 papers in total.) Information extraction is performed with Deepseek-v4-flash and consists of three parts:
   - Observation: the domain problem observed by the paper's authors.
   - Related works: related work surveyed on the basis of the Observation, organized as claims with supporting works. That is, each claim is followed by several papers supporting it.
   - Solution: the solution proposed by the paper's authors, split into an idea part and an implementation part, representing the approach and the concrete implementation respectively.

2. **LLM generation**:
   2.1 The LLM is given the human Observation and asked to output papers in the relevant domain as the supporting works of Related works, together with the claims. To keep style and length consistent, human-related-work results from other papers are included in the prompt for reference. The experiment is run twice: once with retrieval banned in the prompt, and once with retrieval required, producing two outputs.
   2.2 The LLM is given the human Observation and Related works (human / LLM with retrieval banned / LLM with retrieval allowed). The solution is generated three times. To ensure consistent style, length and form, human Solutions of other papers are included in the prompt for reference.
   2.3 The generated results are consolidated into four result files, with the contents shown in the table below:

   | Source | Observation | Related works      | Solution |
   | ------ | ----------- | ------------------ | -------- |
   | Human  | Human       | Human              | Human    |
   | LLM-1  | Human       | Human              | LLM      |
   | LLM-2  | Human       | LLM-with-search    | LLM      |
   | LLM-3  | Human       | LLM-without-search | LLM      |

### 3.1 RQ2-1: Ability of the LLM to Generate Related Works and Core Claim

**Compared objects**: Human & LLM-2 (with-search variant) and Human & LLM-3 (without-search variant). That is, the human related-work set $S_H$ is compared with the $S_L$ generated by each of the two LLM variants.

**Evaluation procedure (per evaluation-guide.md §1)**: first parse the unstructured references in $S_H$ and $S_L$ into (Title, Authors, Year) metadata triples, then call the Semantic Scholar (S2) API to obtain `s2_id`, `doi`, `citationCount`, `venue` and the SPECTER2 vectors; finally, apply a three-level matching algorithm (L1: exact DOI/S2ID match; L2: JaroWinkler(title) ≥ 0.90 and year difference ≤ 1; L3: SPECTER2 cosine similarity ≥ 0.92) to produce a judgment matrix $M_{i,j} \in \{0,1\}$, from which the matched intersection $S_M = S_H \cap S_L$ is obtained. **Papers that S2 cannot retrieve are treated as fabricated/failed records; they are used only to gauge quality and do not participate in matching.**

**Notes on the definition of the RQ2-1 key metrics (RR, W1, Rel in Tables 3-3 / 3-7; the implementation was verified item by item against the evaluation code in 2026-09)**:

- **RR (Recency Ratio, share of papers published in the last 3 years)**: the baseline year is **not the publication year of each observed paper but the fixed evaluation year 2026** (`config.CURRENT_YEAR` takes the year the program is run; window `RECENT_YEARS = 3` years). A cited paper counts as "recent" if and only if `2026 − Year_p ≤ 3` (i.e., published in 2023–2026); RR(S) = number of recent papers / |S|. Papers with missing years cannot be judged and stay in the denominator counted as "non-recent" (the number of papers with valid years on each side is given in the evaluation output). Note that the 8 observed papers themselves were published in different years; a sliding baseline of "within 3 years after the observed paper's publication" would yield different RR values — this experiment uniformly fixes the 2026 baseline to keep the 8 samples comparable.
- **W1 (difference of publication-year distributions, in years)**: within the **same observed paper**, treat the publication years of the human reference set and the LLM reference set as two empirical distributions and compute the absolute integral of the difference between their cumulative distribution functions, W1 = ∫|F_H(x) − F_L(x)|dx. It is a **symmetric, non-negative "distance" quantity with no positive/negative direction of "who is newer/older relative to whom"**: W1 = 0 means the two year distributions are identical (the human and LLM references of that paper have the same old/new structure), and larger W1 means larger distributional difference (e.g., one side concentrated in recent years while the other spans more years or is older). The implementation performs exact integration over discrete years; papers with missing years on either side are removed from the distributions (valid sample counts appear in the evaluation output).
- **Rel (degree of fit to the Observation)**: for each paper p in a reference set S, take its **S2 normalized title + abstract concatenated text** (the SPECTER2 pre-training input format `"title: …\n abstract: …"`; papers without an S2 abstract use only the title) and encode it with SPECTER2 into the paper vector $v_p$; encode that paper's **Observation text** (the human-written observation description, i.e., the Observation in the "Input" of this report) into $v_{\text{obs}}$ with the same encoder. The fit is $\text{Rel}(S, \text{Obs}) = \frac{1}{|S|}\sum_p \cos(v_p, v_{\text{obs}})$. Thus Rel(H)/Rel(L) in Tables 3-3/3-7 measure the overall semantic fit between the human/LLM reference sets and the Observation (the other semantic metrics "intra/inter" are likewise computed on the above title+abstract vectors). The main text uses the encoder `allenai/specter2_base` by default; regarding the concern that "similarities are generally high and weakly discriminative", a three-encoder sensitivity comparison was additionally run with the officially recommended `allenai/specter2` (base + proximity adapter) and `scibert_scivocab_uncased` (independent outputs that do not override the main results), see the **Appendix: Semantic-Encoder Sensitivity Comparison** at the end.

### 3.2 RQ2-2: Solution-Generation Ability from Human Related Work

**Compared objects**: Human & LLM-1. That is, with Observation + human Related Works ($S_H$) as input, the LLM generates a Solution ($\text{Sol}_L$), which is compared with the human Solution ($\text{Sol}_H$).

**Evaluation procedure (per evaluation-guide.md §2)**:
- **Four-dimension Rubric scoring (§2.1)**: an LLM judge scores the solution on four dimensions from 1 to 5 — targetedness ($s_{\text{tgt}}$, whether it precisely addresses the core contradiction of the Observation), technical feasibility ($s_{\text{feas}}$, whether there are flaws in the mathematical/logical/engineering principles), novelty ($s_{\text{nov}}$, whether it goes beyond the mediocre solution of simply stacking baselines), groundedness ($s_{\text{grd}}$, whether it explicitly and reasonably exploits the technical routes in the input $S_H$); the composite quality score is the weighted total, **with equal weights w = 0.25** (i.e., the average of the four dimensions; see config.SOL_WEIGHTS in the evaluation code for the actual run configuration).
- **Semantic embedding similarity (§2.2.1)**: an academic text model (e.g., SciNBERT) is used to embed the solution texts and compute the cosine similarity $\cos(v_{\text{Sol}_L}, v_{\text{Sol}_H})$.
- **AspectRecall of key technical elements (§2.2.2)**: $K$ key technical elements (loss-function improvements, new network modules, heuristic algorithms, etc.) are extracted from $\text{Sol}_H$, and the share covered by $\text{Sol}_L$ is counted.
- In addition, the human Solution is scored under the same in-context rubric as a **human baseline** (§2.1b), so that the LLM scores and the true human level are comparable under the same judge.

### 3.3 RQ2-3: Impact of LLM-RW vs Human-RW on Solution Output, and Attribution

**Compared objects**: LLM-1 & LLM-2 (with-search variant) and LLM-1 & LLM-3 (without-search variant). That is, input A = Observation + LLM-generated Related Works ($S_L$) producing a Solution is compared with input B = Observation + human Related Works ($S_H$) producing a Solution (i.e., the RQ2-2 output), to assess the effect of the two related-work inputs on the quality of the final solution.

**Evaluation procedure (per evaluation-guide.md §3)**:
- **Position-swapped double-blind pairwise evaluation (§3.1)**: to eliminate the position bias of the LLM judge, each sample is evaluated in two rounds (round 1 with the normal A/B positions; round 2 with the positions swapped); a judgment is valid only if the two rounds do not conflict, and a conflict is marked `Tie`. The WinRate is the share of samples in which "the solution from the LLM-RW input wins" across the two-round judgments; the per-dimension score difference is the paired two-round mean of $\Delta_i^{(k)} = \text{Score}^{(k)}(\text{Sol}_{\text{LLM\_RW}}) - \text{Score}^{(k)}(\text{Sol}_{\text{Human\_RW}})$.
- **Cross-judge stability (as actually run)**: the primary judge uses backend qwen, and a cross judge with backend deepseek re-checks the same batch of double-blind tasks; in addition, an **identical-pair control experiment** is run (A and B are the same solution; the ideal output is all Ties) to test whether the judges show position bias or a tendency to manufacture differences.
- **Attribution analysis (§3.2)**: the quality metrics of the LLM-related work from RQ2-1 (Recall, Rel, hallucination rate = 1/Precision) are correlated with the corresponding solution score differences from RQ2-3 via Spearman rank correlation, testing three hypotheses — the retrieval-recall hypothesis (recall → groundedness difference, expected positive), the relevance-transmission hypothesis (Rel → targetedness difference, expected positive), and the hallucination/noise-interference hypothesis (hallucination rate → feasibility difference, expected negative).
- *Note: "Sol_HumanRW" in the two variant files is the same batch of LLM-1 solutions (human-RW input) re-scored in two independent pairwise evaluations; the scores of the same sample fluctuate slightly across the two files (e.g., 52875bf8ef4b3f8c: 3.8750 vs 3.6250), kept as in each evaluation output.*

#### 3.3.3 RQ2-3 Summary

1. **At the preference level (valid verdicts) there is no systematic winner**: after correcting the label-mapping parse, the primary judge qwen produced no two-round-consistent valid verdict on any of the 16 samples across the two variants (apart from 2 samples containing a Tie, all are conflicts of "opposite preferences in the two rounds") — its binary preference points in every round to whichever solution is displayed first, is sensitive to display order, and cannot support a WinRate conclusion. The "qwen WinRate 75.0%, human-RW 0 wins" reported in the original draft was an artifact of this order sensitivity being misread as "consistent wins" due to a label-parsing bug (see the erratum note in this section).
2. **The cross judge deepseek produced a small number of self-consistent valid verdicts**: after the fix, in the with-search variant deepseek chose the LLM-RW-input solution consistently in both rounds for 5dd2d839c66d339a (Win_LLM) and the human-RW-input solution consistently in both rounds for e3d82bafe7cb11e7 (Win_Human); in the without-search variant it likewise chose Win_LLM consistently in both rounds for 5dd2d839c66d339a, with all other samples Tie in both rounds. Its verdicts are self-consistent with its per-round four-dimension scores, and it passed the entire identical-pair control group, so these valid verdicts are credible — but there are too few of them to do more than show that "directionally clear and reproducible preferences exist on individual samples", with one example in each direction; this does not constitute evidence of a systematic advantage.
3. **At the score level (Δ and the §3.1c independent Rubric) there is still a small but consistent difference**: Δ total is positive on all 16 samples (0.25–1.125), in line with the direction of qwen's non-blind independent Rubric; the gain is concentrated in the feasibility and groundedness dimensions — if quality is measured by continuous scoring rather than binary preference, the solutions from the LLM-RW input are slightly ahead. However, qwen's preference output in the same round contradicts its own scores (in round O2 it prefers the human-RW-input solution yet gives the LLM-RW-input solution a higher score), indicating that the two channels should not be conflated.
4. **Attribution**: all 6 Spearman correlations across the two variants are non-significant, and no hypothesis holds with a consistent direction across both variants — no measurable transmission relationship exists between the related-work quality metrics (recall/Rel/hallucination rate) of RQ2-1 and the solution score differences of RQ2-3; the low recall, hallucinations, and other defects of the LLM-related work found in RQ2-1 do **not** materialize as solution-quality loss in RQ2-3.
5. **Mechanistic speculation (stated cautiously)**: combining RQ2-1 (the LLM-RW is semantically well-aligned with the human-RW, with non-negligible quality and topical fit) and RQ2-2 (whatever RW input is used, the LLM solution itself maintains a stable high quality), a self-consistent explanation is that solution quality is mainly determined by the LLM's own generation ability, and differences in the related-work input are "absorbed" before they can affect solution quality; the negative effects of hallucinated references and low recall did not manifest in this batch of samples (deepseek even preferred the human-RW-input solution in both rounds for e3d82bafe7cb11e7 — a counterexample pointing the other way, likewise showing that the effect of input differences is not consistent). This speculation is only an interpretive observation, constrained by the sample size, and was not statistically tested.

### 3.4 Overall Conclusions and Discussion

#### 3.4.1 Answers to the Three Subtask Questions

**RQ2-1 (Can the LLM generate the corresponding Related Works and Core Claim)**: Yes, and with high faithfulness and accurate topical direction — but hard overlap with human-selected references is limited, the output is short, and information completeness is insufficient: the mean recall of the two variants is only 14.8%–15.7% (the human set is much larger than the LLM output; the LLM averages 7.5–8.1 papers per sample vs the human 36), precision is 60.8%–66.4%, and Jaccard is about 0.14; semantically, the centroid cosine with the human reference set is ≥ 0.96 and the degree of fit to the Observation (Rel) is comparable to the human one; the mean log citation count is higher than the human one on all 8 samples in both variants, showing that the LLM prefers citing high-impact references. The Core Claims have NLI factual faithfulness ≥ 0.99 (1 sample in with-search has no valid judgment), logic scores of 3–5, but cover only 8.6%–36.1% of the human atomic assertions. **Enabling retrieval (with-search) brought no improvement in the hard retrieval metrics** (recall 15.7% vs 14.8%, basically the same; precision 60.8% vs 66.4%, actually lower), and the S2 retrieval failure rate was higher (18.5% vs 13.3%; human side 8.0%) — the hallucination proxy rate of the search variant rose rather than fell.

**RQ2-2 (Can the LLM generate a Solution from human related work)**: Yes, and it closely matches the human at the text level — the mean semantic cosine with the human solution is 0.947 (0.938–0.959); the mean weighted total of the four-dimension Rubric is 4.41, above the 4.19 of the human baseline under the same judge (Δ +0.22), with the largest advantages in groundedness (4.375 vs 3.875) and novelty (4.25 vs 4.00), slightly higher feasibility (4.00 vs 3.875), and equal targetedness (5.00 vs 5.00); the per-sample Δ total ranges from 0.00 to +0.50, with 5 samples above the human baseline, 3 tied (5dd2d839c66d339a, c252b8a901feea74, d18c13d036b3708f) and none below. **The main shortcoming is the coverage of key technical elements**: the mean AspectRecall is only 50.6% (0.0%–81.8%), and f948850caef3d25f shows a divergence of "high Rubric score (4.50) + zero element coverage (0/10)".

**RQ2-3 (Impact of LLM-RW vs Human-RW on the Solution output)**: after correcting the label-mapping parse (see the erratum note in §3.3), **no systematic difference was measured at the level of blind-evaluation preference** — the primary judge qwen's binary preference flips with the display order, so all 16 samples of the two variants are judged Tie by the conflict rule; the cross judge deepseek produced only a few two-round-consistent valid verdicts (5dd2d839c66d339a chooses the LLM-RW-input solution in both variants, e3d82bafe7cb11e7 chooses the human-RW-input solution in the with-search variant), which likewise does not constitute evidence of a systematic advantage — e3d82bafe7cb11e7 in fact being a counterexample pointing the other way. **At the score level** (qwen's continuous scoring, independent of preference) the difference is instead consistent and positive everywhere: Δ total is positive on all 16 samples (0.25–1.125), with the gain concentrated in feasibility (differences generally ≥ 0.5, up to +2.0) and groundedness, in line with the direction of the non-blind independent Rubric (§3.1c) — that is, the evidence that "the LLM-RW-input solution is slightly better" comes from scoring rather than pairwise preference. The inter-judge per-sample agreement rate after the fix is 75.0% (with-search) and 87.5% (without-search). **At the attribution level**: all six Spearman correlations are non-significant and their directions are unstable across the two variants; the low recall, hallucinations, and other problems of the LLM-related work found in RQ2-1 were not detected as negative transmission to the quality of the final solutions.

#### 3.4.2 Overall Findings

1. **The intuitive transmission of "related-work quality → solution quality" is not supported by the data**: the samples with the worst quality in RQ2-1 (e.g., b93fea400aedf898: Recall 4.0%, Jaccard 0.04) do not perform badly in RQ2-3, indicating that the quality of LLM-generated solutions is determined more by the LLM's own ability, and that differences in the related-work input are absorbed before they affect the output.
2. **The LLM-output related works are on the whole "small but fine"**: few in number (about one fifth of the human set), with low hard overlap, but high precision, semantically aligned, high citation quality, and a hallucination proxy rate at a controllable low level (13.3%–18.5%); as "context supplied to the LLM for solution generation" they are qualified — consistent with the RQ2-3 results: at the solution-scoring level, using LLM-RW as input is not worse than, and is even slightly better than, using human-RW; at the blind-preference level there is no systematic difference.
3. **At the evaluation-methodology level**: position-swapped double-blind evaluation + identical-pair control + independent Rubric cross-validation constitute an effective judge-audit pipeline — it successfully exposed qwen's property of "binary preference flipping with the display order" (after the corrected parse, no valid winner exists at its preference level), while deepseek's valid verdicts are self-consistent with its scores; the continuous-scoring channel and the preference channel should be interpreted separately. The inter-judge per-sample agreement rate after the fix is 75.0%–87.5% (the 25% in the original draft was caused by an aggregation-parsing bug), but the "winner" evidence remains thin and the conclusions should be extrapolated with caution.

#### 3.4.3 Limitations

1. **Small sample size (N = 8, single data source)**: all statistics and correlations serve only as trend references and have no inferential meaning; the performance of two particular papers (e.g., b93fea400aedf898 and c252b8a901feea74) may dominate some aggregate conclusions.
2. **Judge behavior and the judge-dependence of the conclusions**: after the corrected parse the inter-judge per-sample agreement rate (75.0%–87.5%) is high, but the two judges "discriminate" differently — qwen's binary preference flips with the display order (the preference channel is unusable), so its conclusions rest mainly on the continuous-scoring channel (Δ all positive); deepseek is conservative (87.5%–100% Tie) and gave one counterexample (e3d82bafe7cb11e7 preferring the human-RW-input solution). There are too few valid wins, and the RQ2-3 conclusion of "slightly better at the score level" has not been independently reproduced at the preference level.
3. **Historical defect in aggregation parsing (fixed; archived)**: the win rates/agreement rates of the original evaluation outputs were affected by an o2 label-mapping-reversal bug (the aggregation logic was fixed on 2026-09-07 and regenerated offline from the raw per-round verdicts; see the erratum note in §3.3); all judgment-type numbers in this report and in the evaluation outputs are the fixed versions; for traceability, compare with outputs/reports/_backup_20260907/.
4. **Same-source scoring bias**: the Rubric scores of the human baseline are also given by an LLM judge (same in-context criterion), so the absolute scores contain a judge-preference component; the reading that the LLM solutions "outscore" the human baseline (4.41 vs 4.19) should be kept in check.
5. **Metric-definition boundaries**: an S2 retrieval failure is only a proxy for "fabricated/failed" (it may be related to metadata differences or title/year variants) and is not a strict determination of hallucination; AspectRecall depends on the completeness of the extraction of the human solution's technical elements.
6. **Contradictory trend between failure rate and recall**: the with-search variant has a higher hallucination proxy rate, yet the solution-quality conclusions of the two variants agree, suggesting that under this experimental configuration the retrieval switch neither improved the related work nor damaged the final solutions; the mechanism is still unclear.

### A.4 Interpretation

1. **specter2 (PRX retrieval adapter)**: pulls the embeddings of "related papers" closer together — intra/inter/fit all shift up (Rel mean 0.865→0.900, Intra 0.856→0.887), while substantially boosting the L3 hits of the three-level matching (cosine ≥ 0.92): Recall rises from 15.7% to 24.6% and Precision from 60.8% to 87.6% (Jaccard 0.140→0.232). That is, it is clearly better than base at recognizing "the same paper under a rewritten title/different wording"; but its similarity band is more saturated (mostly 0.88–0.99), so **in absolute terms it does not relieve the "high-similarity" impression**.
2. **scibert**: the similarity band **shifts down and widens overall** — Intra 0.76–0.80 (base 0.85–0.87), Rel 0.78 (base 0.865), Inter 0.96–0.97; the gap between intra and inter (0.78 vs 0.96) is larger than base's, so **the discrimination of "whether a reference is closely tied to the Observation/topic" is clearer**; but L3 hits drop slightly (Recall 14–15%), and the RQ2-2 solution-pair similarities remain 0.934–0.960, almost identical to base.
3. **Common pattern**: all three encoders give high similarities on both "same-topic solution pairs" (RQ2-2, 0.93–0.97) and "related-paper pairs" — indicating that the high absolute values mainly come from **corpus homogeneity** (the 8 observed papers all belong to the ADRS systems-research collection, and both sides of every comparison revolve around the same Observation) and the high relevance of the texts themselves, rather than an encoder defect; the cross-sample **relative differences** (paired differences, H vs L) remain the common basis of interpretation across the three encoders.
4. **Caveat on metric definitions**: Recall/Precision/Jaccard in A.2 are coupled with the guide's L3 threshold of 0.92 (a threshold calibrated in the SPECTER2 semantic space); after switching encoders the meaning of the same threshold changes. Therefore the retrieval metrics and conclusions of the main text §3.1 still use the default specter2_base, and this appendix serves only as sensitivity evidence. Further measures to widen discrimination (e.g., whitening the vectors or introducing cross-encoder calibration) can be left as future work and were not adopted in this experiment.
